# AI Slop

Colloquial term for AI-generated content that is technically competent but soulless — visually or textually coherent, but lacking authenticity, originality, or genuine creative intent. The cultural and consumer backlash against it is now measurable and significant.

---

## Explanation

"AI Slop" describes the flood of AI-generated images, videos, articles, and ads that increasingly saturates social media, advertising, and digital media. The criticism is not that the content is technically bad — it is often technically polished — but that it is indistinguishable, interchangeable, and feels hollow. It triggers what researchers are calling an "authenticity premium" reaction: the more AI content floods a space, the more audiences value and seek out work that is demonstrably human.

**Merriam-Webster named "slop" its word of the year for 2025.**

---

## The Data

- Consumer enthusiasm for AI-generated creator content: **60% (2023) → 26% (2025)** — a 34-point collapse (eMarketer / Billion Dollar Boy)
- Mentions of "AI slop" grew **9x in 2025** vs. 2024, reaching ~2.4 million mentions (Meltwater)
- **82% of sentiment-categorised mentions** online are negative
- **54% of consumers** prefer no AI involvement in creative work (Goldman Sachs, 2025)
- **31% of consumers** say AI in ads makes them *less* likely to choose a brand (CivicScience)
- Engagement with AI-generated articles dropped **40% in 2024** (Olin researchers)
- **The Nuremberg Institute for Market Decisions (2025):** Simply labelling an ad as AI-generated reduces perceived naturalness, purchase intent, and ad attitudes

---

## Corporate Casualties

- **McDonald's Netherlands** pulled its AI-generated Christmas ad in 2025 after intense public backlash
- **OpenAI's Sora mobile app** shut down in early 2026, six months after launch — reportedly costing $1 million/day to operate
- Multiple ad agencies have reported client backlash after AI campaigns underperformed

---

## The Paradox

Even as consumer rejection grew, marketers poured **79% more spending** into AI-generated content in the same period. The industry is spending into a backlash it is simultaneously creating.

---

## What This Means for Entertainment

Rashmith's intuition — that AI-generated movies and content would make audiences bored because "they're already seeing that on social media" — is validated by this data. The [[experience-economy]] and [[immersive-entertainment]] trends are partly a response to AI slop: when digital content becomes indistinguishable and disposable, *physical presence* and *authentic human experience* become the premium.

EY 2026: *"Authenticity and quality become premium assets."*
