# Experience Economy

A theory of economic value progression by [[joseph-pine]] and James H. Gilmore arguing that experiences — not goods or services — are the premium economic layer of the modern era, and that the next frontier is transformations.

---

## Explanation

Pine and Gilmore first published the Experience Economy thesis in the *Harvard Business Review* (1998), then expanded it into *The Experience Economy* (Harvard Business School Press, 1999; updated 2020).

**Their progression of economic value:**

| Stage | Example | What is sold |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| Commodities | Raw coffee beans | Raw materials |
| Goods | Ground coffee in a bag | Physical products |
| Services | Coffee made at a diner | Activities performed |
| **Experiences** | Starbucks | Memorable events staged |
| **Transformations** | Coaching, education | Genuine personal change |

Their core argument: as goods and services become commoditised, businesses that *stage experiences* — turning consumption into a memorable event — command premium pricing and deeper loyalty.

---

## Why It Matters

This framework is the theoretical backbone of the [[immersive-entertainment]] revolution. Netflix House, Punchdrunk's *Sleep No More*, teamLab, Meow Wolf — these are all direct, billion-dollar operationalisations of a 1998 thesis.

In 2025–2026, every major entertainment consultancy is citing Pine and Gilmore:
- **EY** frames experiential businesses as "strategic necessity, not adjacent opportunity"
- **McKinsey** uses it to frame the Gen Z location-based entertainment shift
- **Gensler/IEI** uses it as the explicit theoretical foundation of their Immersive Industry Report
- **[[charles-melcher]]** cites it throughout *The Future of Storytelling*

---

## The Transformation Layer

Pine and Gilmore's follow-up work argues the next shift goes beyond experiences to **Transformations** — offerings that genuinely change the customer's life. This aligns with the direction immersive entertainment is heading: not just memorable, but personally meaningful.

---

## Connection to Entertainment Disruption

The collapse of passive entertainment (declining cinema attendance, Gen Z disengagement from traditional sports broadcasts) makes sense through this lens: passive viewing was always a *service* offering. The market is now demanding *experiences* and eventually *transformations*. Studios and leagues that don't make this shift will be commoditised by streaming and social media.
