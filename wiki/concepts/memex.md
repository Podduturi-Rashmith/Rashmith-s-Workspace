# Memex

A hypothetical personal knowledge device proposed by [[vannevar-bush]] in his 1945 essay "As We May Think" — widely regarded as the conceptual ancestor of hypertext, personal computing, and modern PKM systems including the [[llm-wiki-pattern]].

---

## Explanation

The Memex (from "memory extender") was Bush's vision of a desk-sized device on which an individual could store all their books, records, and communications, and retrieve any item "with exceeding speed and flexibility." Its defining feature was "associative trails" — links between documents that the user could create, follow, and share, mimicking the associative leaps of human thought rather than the rigid hierarchies of library catalogues.

---

## Why It Matters

The Memex anticipated hypertext (Ted Nelson, Tim Berners-Lee), personal computing, and modern PKM. Its core insight — that connections between documents are as valuable as the documents themselves — remains the animating principle behind tools like [[obsidian]] and concepts like [[second-brain]]. Bush's unsolved problem was who creates and maintains those connections at scale. The [[llm-wiki-pattern]] is one answer: the LLM does.

---

## How Sources Treat This Concept

Both [[andrej-karpathy]] (via embedded text in [[defileo-claude-obsidian-second-brain]]) and [[defileo]] directly invoke the Memex as the spiritual predecessor to the LLM wiki pattern. The framing is consistent: Bush had the right vision in 1945, but couldn't solve the maintenance problem. LLMs solve it now.
