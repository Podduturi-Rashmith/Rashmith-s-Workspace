# Second Brain

A personal external knowledge system — digital or physical — that captures, organises, and makes retrievable everything a person reads, thinks, and learns, so that accumulated knowledge compounds over time rather than being forgotten.

---

## Explanation

The "second brain" concept (popularised by Tiago Forte) frames an external note-taking system as an extension of human memory. The goal is to capture ideas and information at the moment of encounter, organise them in a structure that enables retrieval and synthesis, and build up a personal knowledge base that grows more useful over time. Common implementations use tools like Notion, Obsidian, Roam Research, or paper notebooks.

---

## Why It Matters

Human memory is lossy and non-searchable. Books, articles, podcasts, and meetings produce insights that fade within days. A second brain externalises knowledge so it persists, can be searched and connected, and can be built upon rather than re-derived from scratch. The challenge has always been maintenance: most second-brain systems decay because keeping them organised requires more discipline than most people sustain.

---

## How Sources Treat This Concept

[[defileo-claude-obsidian-second-brain]] frames the [[llm-wiki-pattern]] (Claude + Obsidian) as the definitive solution to second-brain decay. The argument: every previous second-brain system fails for the same reason — maintenance burden outpaces value created. The LLM-maintained wiki eliminates this by making maintenance a single command. The human curates and thinks; Claude files and cross-references.

Leo's framing positions the LLM-maintained vault as a second brain that "never sleeps, never forgets, and gets smarter every single day" — a qualitative upgrade over passive note-taking systems.
