# Future of Entertainment: What Experts Are Saying (2025–2026)

*Research compiled in response to: "According to the experts what is the future of entertainment? Is this the end? Where is the new beginning?"*

---

## The Short Answer

The old model — passive, screen-based, franchise-driven entertainment — is structurally declining. A new model is emerging: **immersive, experiential, participatory, live, and social.** Every major consultancy (EY, Deloitte, McKinsey, PwC) and every major operator (Netflix, Disney, the NBA) is pointing in the same direction.

This is not the end of entertainment. It is the end of *watching* entertainment and the beginning of *being inside* it.

---

## 1. AI Content Saturation — The "Slop" Problem

Consumer enthusiasm for AI-generated content **collapsed from 60% (2023) to 26% (2025)** — a 34-point drop in two years (eMarketer / Billion Dollar Boy).

- The term **"AI Slop"** — content that is technically competent but soulless — grew 9x in mentions in 2025, reaching 2.4 million references. **Merriam-Webster named "slop" its word of the year for 2025.**
- **82% of sentiment-categorised mentions** of AI slop online are negative (Meltwater, 2025)
- **31% of consumers** say AI in ads makes them *less* likely to choose a brand (CivicScience)
- **54% of consumers** prefer no AI involvement in creative work at all (Goldman Sachs)
- Engagement with AI-generated articles dropped **40% in 2024** (Olin researchers)
- McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI-generated Christmas ad in 2025 after public backlash

**EY's 2026 Media & Entertainment Trends Report** (Javi Borges & John Harrison, EY Global Media & Entertainment Leaders):
> *"Authenticity and quality become premium assets."*

**The paradox:** Marketers poured **79% more spending** into AI-generated content even as consumer rejection grew. The industry is spending into a backlash.

**Sam Altman** called OpenAI's Sora "the ChatGPT for creativity." OpenAI then shut the Sora mobile app down in early 2026 — six months after launch — reportedly because it cost **$1 million per day** to run.

## 2. Franchise and Remake Fatigue — The Data

- In 2024 for the **first time in 50 years**, all top 10 highest-grossing films were sequels
- Yet US domestic box office totalled **$8.7 billion in 2025** — down 20%+ from the 2019 peak
- Sequels released: 26 in 2024 → 34 in 2025 — more sequels, less money

**Specific underperformance:**

| Film | Revenue |
|------|---------|
| Avatar: The Way of Water (2022) | $688.8M domestic |
| Avatar: Fire and Ash (2024) | $378.5M domestic — **45% drop** |
| Wicked (2024) | $475M domestic |
| Wicked: For Good (2025) | ~$350M domestic — **26% drop** |

**Alicia Reese**, SVP Equity Research, Wedbush Securities:
> *"It's never going to be a home run from here on out, because people are pickier than they used to be."*

**Jeff Bock**, Senior Box Office Analyst, Exhibitor Relations:
> *"Sometimes an industry has to take two steps backwards before going forward again."*

CNBC (January 2026): The problem is self-reinforcing — fewer original films get theatrical releases, which means fewer originals prove themselves, which means studios keep making sequels.

## 3. The End of Passive Entertainment

**Ted Sarandos**, co-CEO of Netflix, at the Time100 Summit (April 2025):
> *"The movie theater model is an outmoded idea — for most people, not for everybody."*

**Charles Melcher**, founder of the Future of Storytelling Summit, in *The Future of Storytelling* (Artisan Books, 2025):
> *"These days we don't want to just watch our media — we want to be a part of it. To touch, feel, and test our surroundings, to make decisions that directly affect the story's outcome. Immersive events are growing at such a staggering rate that their revenue will soon outpace film and television combined."*

Melcher calls the new form **"living stories"** — experiences that are agentic, immersive, embodied, responsive, social, and transformative.

**Gensler Research Institute + Immersive Experience Institute** (2025 Immersive Industry Report):
> *"While traditional, passive forms of media and live performance are largely stagnant or declining, location-based performance, video games, and other forms of experience that center participant engagement and interactivity continue to grow — in some cases almost doubling."*

### Who Is Already Doing This

**Netflix House** — Netflix's permanent experiential venues:
- Philadelphia (opened Nov 2024, 100,000+ sq ft)
- Dallas and King of Prussia (2025)
- Las Vegas (2027)
- CEO Sarandos: *"The next generation of what a theme park might be"* — venues people visit 4–5 times a year, not once every 5 years

**Punchdrunk's Sleep No More** — immersive theatre running 9 consecutive years in New York, still sells out. Shanghai production: **$75 million revenue, 620,000+ participants.**

**teamLab** — immersive digital art spaces. **4.2 million visitors** to its two Tokyo museums in 2025 alone.

**Meow Wolf** — experiential art installations across the US; expanding rapidly.

**The Bridgerton Experience** — IP-driven immersive event launched in 11 cities across 3 countries; **150,000+ visitors** within months (McKinsey case study).

### The Gen Z Data Point That Changes Everything

**McKinsey (January 2026):** Gen Z is **4 times more likely than older generations** to attend an immersive experience. The generation most disengaged from passive TV and cinema is the most engaged with participatory experiences. They haven't abandoned entertainment — they've abandoned *watching.*

**52% of all people** now attend 6+ immersive experiences per year. **73%** will travel specifically for an experience.

## 5. Sports — The Expert View

**Deloitte 2026 Sports Industry Outlook** (Kat Harwood, Chad Deweese, Tim Bridge, Jeff Harris):
- AI becoming "foundational for growth" as an intelligence layer across operations
- Fans now expect AI-powered personalised experiences, flexible pricing, location-based promotions
- At least **half of major league teams** will operate within mixed-use districts within a decade
- The stadium as **year-round platform**, not single-use sports venue

**NBA + Disney/FOX:** Live game broadcasts featuring animated characters integrated into the action.

**ESPN + DraftKings:** Betting lines and odds embedded directly into broadcasts (effective Dec 2025) — gambling becoming part of the viewing experience.

**Creator Sports networks:** Generating **3–4x the engagement** of traditional play-by-play formats when hosted by fan-favourite creators.

**SVG Europe 2026 Predictions:** "XR consumption will expand beyond experiments into the mainstream — digital lounges, virtual stadiums, and interactive rooms allowing fans to be present at events without attending."

## 6. The Experience Economy — The Theoretical Foundation

**B. Joseph Pine II and James H. Gilmore** first articulated the Experience Economy in the *Harvard Business Review* (1998), then in their book *The Experience Economy* (Harvard Business School Press, 1999; updated 2020).

**Their progression of economic value:**
> Commodities → Goods → Services → **Experiences** → Transformations

The thesis: when goods and services become commoditised, experiences become the premium economic layer. Their follow-up work argues the next shift is to **Transformations** — offerings that genuinely change the customer.

**Who is citing this in 2025–2026:**
- EY 2026 Media & Entertainment Report frames experiential businesses as "strategic necessity, not adjacent opportunity"
- McKinsey's location-based entertainment research uses it to frame the Gen Z shift
- Gensler/IEI Immersive Report uses it as the explicit theoretical foundation
- Charles Melcher cites Pine and Gilmore throughout his book

**Netflix House, teamLab, Meow Wolf, Sleep No More** are all direct, billion-dollar operationalisations of Pine and Gilmore's 1998 thesis.

## 7. The Creator Economy — A Second Hollywood Emerging

**Brent Weinstein**, Head of Digital Media at CAA:
> *"2025 was the year creators went fully mainstream."*

- Ms. Rachel → Netflix
- The NFL and the Oscars → YouTube
- Bari Weiss' The Free Press → Paramount
- Creator-owned studios (Dhar Mann, Alan Chikin Chow) building Disney-quality production facilities

The creator economy is not replacing Hollywood — it is **building alongside it**, with different economics, different ownership, and different audience relationships.

## The Synthesis — Radio → TV → Experience

| Era | Format | Audience Relationship |
|-----|--------|----------------------|
| Radio | Audio only | Imagination-driven, intimate |
| TV | Passive visual | One-way, broadcast to many |
| **Now emerging** | Immersive, live, participatory | **You are inside it** |

Every expert, every data point, every major corporate bet points at the same transition. The new beginning is not a better screen. It is **no screen at all** — or screens that dissolve the boundary between you and the story.
